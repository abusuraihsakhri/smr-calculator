Method and assumptions

For indirect standardization, expected deaths are E = Σ(population or person-time in stratum × reference mortality rate); overall SMR = ΣO / ΣE. The ratio may also be shown multiplied by 100. The Garwood two-sided Poisson interval uses χ² quantiles: lower bound = χ²α/2, 2O / (2E) for O > 0 (otherwise 0); upper bound = χ²1−α/2, 2(O+1) / (2E).

Interpretation and limitations

SMR > 1 implies more observed deaths than expected; SMR < 1 implies fewer. The interval describes sampling uncertainty in observed counts, conditional on fixed expected deaths. It does not account for uncertainty in reference rates, confounding, selection or the appropriateness of a standard population. SMRs from differently standardized populations may not be directly comparable. This is an analytic research tool, not a clinical diagnosis or decision system.

Methodology: Liddell (1984), simple exact analysis of the standardized mortality ratio. No external libraries, telemetry, server calls, or uploaded data storage are used in the calculator.
